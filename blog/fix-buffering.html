Why your stream keeps buffering (and how to fix it)

Nothing kills movie night like a spinning wheel every two minutes. Here's the good news our experts share on almost every call: buffering is a network problem nine times out of ten, not a broken TV. Run these six checks in order before you buy anything new.

1. Check your actual speed at the TV

Run a speed test on your phone while standing next to the TV. For smooth 4K you want at least 25 Mbps at that spot. The speed near your front door doesn't count. Under 10 Mbps and you'll buffer no matter what you tweak.

2. Switch to the 5 GHz Wi-Fi band

Most routers broadcast two networks. The 5 GHz one is dramatically faster for streaming at short range. If your stick or TV joined the slower 2.4 GHz band during setup, move it over in the network settings.

3. Restart everything in the right order

Unplug the TV/stick and the router. Wait 60 seconds. Plug the router back in, wait for its lights to settle, then power up the TV. This clears stale routes that cause most "worked yesterday" cases.

4. Clear the app's cache

Open the app's settings (or your device's application settings) and clear cached data. Bloated caches are a classic cause of one app buffering while others play fine.

5. Lower the resolution test

Drop playback to 1080p temporarily. If buffering stops, your connection can't sustain 4K right now. A settings and placement fix, not new hardware.

6. Move the router or the TV closer

Every wall between router and TV roughly halves real-world speed. Even moving the stick's HDMI extender or shifting the router one room closer can end buffering for good.
